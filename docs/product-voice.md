# UNEEM product voice

UNEEM should feel fast to read and easy to act on. Users should not need to read paragraphs to understand a screen.

## Core rules

- Prefer the action over an explanation.
- Titles are usually 2–5 words.
- Use a subtitle only when it adds information the UI cannot show by itself.
- Keep helper text to one short sentence.
- Buttons use direct verbs: `Continue`, `Book`, `Join`, `Retry`, `Resubmit`.
- Errors say what happened and what the user can do next.
- Do not repeat the same rule on several parts of one screen.
- Do not expose database, moderation, or workflow terminology to students.
- Avoid marketing copy inside task screens.
- Avoid technical reassurance such as “without extra steps” or “authoritative state”. Show the better experience instead.

## Voice

Short. Calm. Friendly. Action-first.

UNEEM is a university sports product, not a SaaS dashboard. Copy should sound natural to a student trying to play, book, join, verify an account, or get help.

## Examples

Prefer:
- `3 spots left`
- `You're in!`
- `Couldn't load facilities.`
- `We're reviewing your student card.`
- `Fix your Student ID and resubmit.`

Avoid:
- `This match currently has three remaining participant positions available.`
- `Your verification request is currently under administrative review.`
- `Facilities are unavailable right now. Retry without losing your place.`

## Screen responsibility

- Login: sign in. No product explanation.
- Signup: explain only the difference between university-email and personal-email verification.
- Verification: explain what is needed and the next action.
- Home: help the student decide what to play/book/join now.
- Match: show time, place, people, spots, and one clear action.
- Restrictions: reason, what is blocked, how to fix or appeal.
- Admin: operational language, concise and specific; no raw technical codes in user-facing copy.

## Arabic

Use simple modern Arabic. Keep security, verification, and moderation copy precise. Sports/home copy can be warmer and more conversational, but avoid switching randomly between heavy Darija and formal MSA on the same flow.

Do not translate word-for-word when a shorter natural Arabic phrase is clearer.
